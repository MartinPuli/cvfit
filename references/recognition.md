# When the reader has never heard of your employer

Silver.dev places Argentine and Uruguayan engineers into US startups. Their
guidance on what goes wrong is blunt and it is the single most useful rule for
anyone applying across a border:

> LatAm engineers' resumes rarely feature recognizable US company names. Instead
> of pedigree, focus on project impact, tenure at startups, and communication
> clarity.

A recruiter in San Francisco reading "Script S.A." learns nothing. The same line
reading "Script S.A. (BBVA, Volkswagen Financial Services)" tells them the
candidate worked on systems for a major bank and an automotive lender, which is
the fact that mattered all along.

## The rule

For every organisation on the page, ask whether the reader will recognise the
name. When the answer is no, say what it is in the smallest number of words that
carry the weight:

- the client or parent, when that is the recognisable part:
  `Script S.A. (BBVA, Volkswagen Financial Services)`
- the rank, when there is one:
  `the second largest private bank in Argentina`
- the category and scale, when neither applies:
  `a wholesale distributor moving 40,000 SKUs`

Do this in the entry title or the first bullet, never in a separate glossary.

## What replaces pedigree

Three things carry a resume that has no famous logos on it:

**Impact with a denominator.** `1st of 151 projects` travels; `won a hackathon`
does not. `Cut manual migration effort roughly 40%` travels; `improved
efficiency` does not.

**Tenure.** Two years at one company reads as trust earned. Four six-month stints
read as a question. When tenure is short, the bullets have to work harder.

**Communication clarity.** For a remote role across time zones this is being
assessed from the resume itself, before any interview. Long sentences and vague
verbs are evidence against the candidate, not just bad style.
